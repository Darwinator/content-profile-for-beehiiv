# Naming

Load this reference only when the current job is a working title, publication identity, or how the newsletter relates to an existing business name. Do not open a naming workshop during the five-question kickoff or after every brief.

A name is a reversible working title until the founder makes it consequential. It cannot block the first issue.

## When to name

- After founder, reader, territories, publication kind, and any business relationship are known.
- If a name or lockup already exists, use it and move on.
- If the founder is unsure, pick a working title or defer and continue. Do not invent a brand world.

## Two branches

### Named business or product exists

Recommend a relationship, then a working title.

1. Distinct publication name endorsed by the business — useful when the newsletter should be broader than the product.
2. Use the business name as the publication.
3. Completely independent name.
4. Defer until after the first issue.
5. Other.

Do not drop the chosen relationship when you later list titles. If they chose “endorsed by the business,” lockups keep that form.

### No business or product name yet

Do not invent an “endorsed by X” question.

Ask whether they already have a name or would rather use a working title and revisit it after the first issue.

Recommend defer when naming is consuming momentum. Options: I already have a name / a working title is fine / name it later / Other.

## What to recommend

Prefer **one working title** plus an optional subtitle, not a six-name agency slate.

A safe first-issue recommendation:

- easy to say and spell;
- understandable enough that a stranger can infer the beat or reader from the title, subtitle, or surrounding lockup;
- distinct from obvious same-category lookalikes;
- consistent with the chosen relationship to the business; and
- reversible while the publication learns.

This does not require a place, job, or founder name. **Choose the pattern that fits the publication kind:**

- a local/media publication may benefit from a place cue;
- a practitioner or creator publication may use the founder's name when they are the recognizable face of the work;
- a company publication may use or be endorsed by the business name;
- a standalone publication may need its own descriptive or suggestive identity.

If several candidates are the same pattern with swapped nouns, pick one and drop the rest. Stop after **two short slates**. A third only if the founder asks and none of the first set felt like them. Then stop: use a working title or remain unnamed until issue one.

## How to talk about it

- Say “working title,” “we’ll use this for now,” or “this can change after the first issue.”
- Do not say “provisional default.”
- Do not present trademark, domain, or handle clearance you have not done. A noisy search is an editorial screen, not legal advice.
- Do not use sound symbolism, rhyme, or naming folklore as proof that a title will grow the list.
- Do not write a persona into the name from one thin voice signal.

## Evidence boundary

There is no study that randomly assigns newsletter titles and measures subscribers, opens, or forwards. Do not claim a name will grow the list.

Current first-party beehiiv guidance is broader than one naming formula: it suggests using a personal name for freelancers, professionals, or people who are the face of the brand, alongside other creative approaches. Treat it as platform guidance, not an outcome guarantee.

## Output shape

When naming is the job:

1. State the relationship branch (business / no business).
2. Recommend one working title and why, in one or two sentences.
3. Offer at most two alternatives plus Other.
4. Give each a one-line risk, not a lockup essay.
5. Ask which to use for now, and continue to the next launch gate.

If they dislike the set, offer one more slate or defer. Do not keep inventing worlds.
