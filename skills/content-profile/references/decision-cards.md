# Decision Cards — Founder-Led Anchors

Six maintained mechanism cases from verified public research (29-case corpus, 2026-08-07; public evidence only, no employer-confidential information). These are **decision evidence, not imitation targets**. Use a card's transferable rule to shape a recommendation and name the rule when you apply it. Never tell a founder to write like a named operator, copy a voice, visual identity, or persona.

When a founder asks what a recommendation is based on, cite the relevant card and its public source honestly. If no card fits the founder's situation, say so and fall back to bounded live research — do not force-fit an anchor.

Provenance is part of the product: when a card shapes a recommendation, selection, or draft structure, the card ID must appear in the saved artifact (options file, issue brief, decision log) alongside the transferable rule, not only in conversational reasoning. An undecided founder is not an exception — an "I don't know yet" founder deserves *more* visible card provenance, because the cards are doing the deciding the founder cannot yet do. If the honest answer is "no card fits," write that in the artifact too.

## CARD-01 — Greg's Letter (Greg Isenberg / Late Checkout)

- **Situation:** portfolio operator with many concurrent experiments; risk of breadth outrunning evidence.
- **What they did:** each issue connects one live operator experiment (e.g. a $65 ad + Typeform demand test) to a general startup mechanism, with inputs, decisions, result, and caveat. Weekly.
- **Transferable rule:** connect one concrete experiment to one general mechanism; show the receipts and the caveat. A tested case beats three provocative concepts.
- **Do not copy:** the rapid-fire startup-idea voice or packaging; presenting unverified portfolio metrics as fact.
- **Failure test:** if an issue's mechanism would survive with the receipts deleted, it is a hot take, not an operator issue.
- **Best fit:** founders whose work produces frequent small experiments.
- **Source:** https://www.gregisenberg.com/ ; https://latecheckout.substack.com/p/the-best-65-ive-ever-spent — verified 2026-08-07.

## CARD-02 — The Curiosity Chronicle (Sahil Bloom)

- **Situation:** broad-interest operator whose topics could sprawl past any promise.
- **What they did:** recurring issue types under one umbrella promise; broad curiosity converted into named, reusable frameworks; formats evolved without losing the core reader outcome. Scaled to 4×/week only after years, archive depth, and team capacity.
- **Transferable rule:** recurring containers under one umbrella promise let topics vary without breaking reader trust. High cadence is a scaled exception — default a solo founder to weekly.
- **Do not copy:** the personal-development voice, framework names, or the 4×/week cadence as a starting benchmark.
- **Failure test:** if a new reader cannot state the promise after two issues, the umbrella has failed.
- **Best fit:** founders worried their interests are "too broad."
- **Source:** https://www.sahilbloom.com/newsletter ; https://x.com/SahilBloom/status/1628092486009618432 — verified 2026-08-07.

## CARD-03 — The Bootstrapped Founder (Arvid Kahl)

- **Situation:** solo builder with limited weekly writing time.
- **What they did:** one weekly research/writing artifact feeds newsletter, blog, podcast, and archive; a standing promise ("no sketchy hacks"); tools and affiliates disclosed; operating evidence kept distinct from generalized advice.
- **Transferable rule:** produce one strong unit and reuse it across formats; disclose commercial relationships; label what is experience versus advice. Consistency compounds — his audience came from a year of unbroken weekly output, not virality.
- **Do not copy:** the "calm founder" voice; revealing competitively sensitive details just to seem transparent; affiliate-stuffed issues.
- **Failure test:** if sustaining cadence requires manufacturing filler, cut cadence, not quality.
- **Best fit:** time-constrained technical founders.
- **Source:** https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/newsletter/ ; https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/the-bootstrapped-founder-by-arvid — verified 2026-08-07.

## CARD-04 — Nik Sharma's Sunday Newsletter

- **Situation:** agency/service operator whose paid work is the content source.
- **What they did:** mines each week's real client work for one reader-relevant problem; compresses it into a short tactical issue (~4 hours); includes what failed, not just wins; names clients only when public and authorized.
- **Transferable rule:** one week of real work contains one issue. Compression is the craft: one problem, one tactic, honest failure notes. The newsletter demonstrates the judgment the business sells.
- **Do not copy:** the persona; exposing client-confidential details; passing a promotional case off as neutral benchmark.
- **Failure test:** if the issue is useful only as an ad for the founder's services, it fails the independent-value gate.
- **Best fit:** consultants and service founders with recurring client problems.
- **Source:** https://www.nik.co/subscribe ; https://x.com/mrsharma/status/1674177859961729024 — verified 2026-08-07.

## CARD-05 — Founding Journey (Michael Houck)

- **Situation:** founder treating the newsletter itself as a business.
- **What they did:** clarified a real reader job first, surveyed readers, tested acquisition channels one at a time, sequenced monetization (sponsorships → paid tier → community → products) instead of launching everything at once; measured subscriber quality, not just volume.
- **Transferable rule:** sequence ambition — prove one reader job and one channel before adding monetization layers. Also an operations warning: keep canonical URL, platform, cadence copy, and archive identity synchronized across surfaces; his parallel beehiiv/Substack split shows how drift confuses readers.
- **Do not copy:** aggressive lead-magnet mechanics or heavy paid acquisition without economics and consent; assuming his cash-intensive growth loop fits a beginner.
- **Failure test:** if growth spend is planned before a repeatable issue exists, the sequence is inverted.
- **Best fit:** founders with media-business ambitions.
- **Source:** https://foundingjourney.com/ ; https://www.passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/turn-your-newsletter-into-a-business-with-michael-houck — verified 2026-08-07.

## CARD-06 — Big Desk Energy (Tyler Denk / beehiiv)

- **Situation:** founder whose company is itself the story; adjacency risk of every issue becoming a press release.
- **What they did:** mines current operations for real receipts; translates one company decision (e.g. "Build vs Buy") into a general founder lesson; disclosed the operator/company relationship plainly; recurring sections; complete independent value before sponsor/product modules.
- **Transferable rule:** company proximity is the advantage only when the decision is translated into a lesson the reader can use at their own company. Disclose the relationship; deliver full value before any offer.
- **Do not copy:** the slang, humor, vibes, or music sections; treating company milestones as neutral analysis; using its scale (100K+ subs, self-reported) as a cold-start benchmark.
- **Failure test:** if the lesson evaporates when the company name is swapped out, the issue is a press release.
- **Best fit:** founders building in public with a live product.
- **Source:** https://mail.bigdeskenergy.com/ (origin/strategy essay "What is Big Desk Energy," 2024-11-05, in the archive) ; https://mail.bigdeskenergy.com/p/build-vs-buy — verified 2026-08-07.

## Cross-anchor rules (Track 1 synthesis)

1. Write from current operating proximity; receipts are the moat no generic content engine can manufacture.
2. Make the reader promise narrower than the founder's full life.
3. Turn one concrete case into one general mechanism per issue.
4. Place business offers after independent value; disclose sponsors, affiliates, and the operator/company relationship.
5. Weekly is the center of gravity for a solo operator; higher cadence is a scaled exception, not a target.
6. Anti-patterns to name when rejecting an idea: founder diary with no reader outcome; disguised press release; unverified growth claims; copied personality; client-confidential leakage; cadence scaled before the unit is repeatable.

## Maintenance

Cards derive from the verified 2026-08-07 research corpus. When re-verifying, update the date; when an operator materially changes their publication, revise or retire the card rather than letting it silently go stale. New cards require the same standard: public evidence, mechanism extraction, an explicit do-not-copy line, and a failure test.
