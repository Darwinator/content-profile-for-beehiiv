# Promotion — The First 25 Readers

Load this when the launch checklist's promotion gate is current, when the founder asks "how do I get readers?", or right after the first send. Do not open a growth workshop during kickoff or before the first issue exists.

The V1 promotion job is small and honest: help a founder get their first 25 real readers without spamming anyone, buying anything, or building a funnel. Growth machinery is a later, evidence-justified conversation.

## Ground rules

- Promotion advice must fit this founder: their audience, existing presence, strengths, and available time. Never hand over a generic channel checklist.
- Recommend one primary channel plus one supporting motion — not five channels. Consistency on one beats fragments on many (CARD-03: Kahl's audience came from a year of unbroken weekly output and X calls-to-action, not virality).
- Test channels one at a time and judge before adding another (CARD-05: Houck sequenced acquisition; parallel everything is a scaled operation's game).
- Reader quality over count. Replies, forwards, and "this was useful" beat raw subscribers (CARD-01: engagement-over-volume; a small committed list is the goal at this stage).
- Never recommend paid acquisition, engagement-bait mechanics, or mass cold outreach at this stage.

## The first-25 sequence

1. **Personal invitations, not a blast.** 10–20 individually sent notes to people who plausibly match the reader definition: what the publication is, why this person specifically, one line of the promise, the signup link. No BCC lists, no "announcing my newsletter!" guilt subscriptions — those inflate the count and poison early signal.
2. **One community they are already in.** A Slack/Discord/forum/subreddit/local group where the founder already participates. Share the first issue where it genuinely answers that community's interest, following the community's self-promotion norms. Participating members can share; strangers doing drive-bys get removed.
3. **The founder's strongest existing channel.** Where do they already have any presence — X, LinkedIn, a podcast guest spot, an email signature, a customer list they may appropriately use? One channel, matched to where the reader actually is. A DTC founder's readers are not on the founder's private Instagram; a B2B consultant's readers may well be on LinkedIn.
4. **The issue itself as the asset.** Each send should be worth forwarding. A specific, useful issue plus a one-line "know someone dealing with this? forward it" is the most durable early loop.
5. **Ask the readers who respond.** When someone replies positively, it is reasonable to ask whether one or two people they know would find it useful. Person-to-person, never automated.

## What to record

In the launch checklist and decision log: the chosen primary channel and why it fits, the supporting motion, and the review point (after ~3 issues: any replies? any forwards? is the channel producing readers who match the definition?). If the channel produces nothing after a fair test, change the channel, not the publication.

## What comes later (not V1 defaults)

Referral programs, recommendations networks, cross-promotion swaps, lead magnets, paid growth, and SEO are real tools that earn their complexity only after the publication has a proven issue loop and evidence about who actually reads it. Recommend them only in response to observed need and stage — never because they exist (CARD-05: sequence ambition; CARD-06: full value before any machinery).
