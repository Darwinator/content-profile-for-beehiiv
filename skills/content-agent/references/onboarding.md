# Onboarding Procedure

Onboarding creates a usable first model of the publication, not a permanent diagnosis of the founder. Keep it conversational, bounded, and correctable.

## Outcome

Produce two founder-confirmed private records:

- `publication-brief.md`: reader, promise, publication stage, business relationship, cadence, territories, and evidence access.
- `voice-and-boundaries.md`: desired voice, exclusions, confidentiality, disclosure needs, and publishing authority.

Do not write founder answers into this reference or any other distribution-owned file.

## Conversation sequence

Ask one focused question at a time. Use the answer to choose the next question rather than reading a questionnaire verbatim.

1. **Reader:** Who is this for, and what situation are they in when the issue reaches them?
2. **Useful change:** What should a good issue help that reader understand, decide, or do?
3. **Founder proximity:** What work, customers, experiments, decisions, mistakes, or results give the founder unusually close evidence?
4. **Business relationship:** What business does the publication support, and what commercial relationship would still preserve reader trust?
5. **Existing publication:** What has already been sent, promised, or rejected? What has readers responded to?
6. **Operating reality:** What cadence is genuinely sustainable? What inputs are naturally available?
7. **Boundaries:** What is confidential, off-limits, legally sensitive, employer-owned, or likely to require disclosure or proof?
8. **Voice:** What should the writing feel like, and which habits or tones should it avoid?

For an existing publication, ask for a small representative sample rather than an indiscriminate archive: the welcome email, two or three issues the founder considers representative, and any issue they regret or would now handle differently.

## Confirmation discipline

During synthesis, label each item as one of:

- **Founder-confirmed fact or decision**
- **Agent inference awaiting confirmation**
- **Open question**

Present the smallest meaningful summary first. Ask the founder to correct omissions and false assumptions. Only after explicit confirmation:

1. update the private publication brief and boundary record;
2. add a decision-log entry recording what was confirmed and when;
3. preserve unresolved questions visibly rather than guessing.

## Anti-patterns

- Asking for a complete company history before providing value.
- Treating aspirational cadence as operating reality.
- Equating the buyer with the newsletter reader without checking.
- Extracting a voice profile from one sample and calling it confirmed.
- Promising a content calendar before understanding the publication.
- Recommending segmentation, automations, multiple editions, or a welcome sequence because they are available rather than needed.
- Turning private onboarding details into shared product learning.

## Completion check

Onboarding is complete enough for the first cycle when the agent can answer:

- Who should find this useful?
- What recurring promise is being tested?
- Why is the founder close enough to say something credible?
- How may the business appropriately appear?
- What evidence is available?
- What must not be used?
- What cadence can actually be sustained?
- Which statements remain unconfirmed?
