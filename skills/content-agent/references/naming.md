# Naming

Load this reference only when the current job is a working title, publication identity, or how the newsletter relates to an existing business name. Do not open a naming workshop during the five-question kickoff or after every brief.

A name is a reversible working title until the founder makes it consequential. It cannot block the first issue.

## When to name

- After founder, reader, territories, and (if it exists) the business relationship are known.
- If a name or lockup already exists, use it and move on.
- If the founder is unsure, pick a working title and continue. Do not invent a brand world.

## Two branches

### Named business or product exists

Recommend a relationship, then a working title.

1. Distinct publication name endorsed by the business — usually the recommendation when the newsletter should be broader than the product.
2. Use the business name as the publication.
3. Completely independent name.
4. Defer until after the first issue.
5. Other.

Do not drop the chosen relationship when you later list titles. If they chose “endorsed by the business,” lockups keep that form.

### No business or product name yet

Do not invent an “endorsed by X” question.

Ask: do they already have a name, or should we use a working title and name it after the first issue?

Recommend defer. Options: I already have a name / a working title is fine / name it later / Other.

## What to recommend

Prefer **one working title** plus a subtitle, not a six-name agency slate.

Safe default for a first issue:

- one or two words, easy to say and spell;
- place or job in the **name or the subtitle**, not neither;
- a stranger can guess the beat in one try;
- **suggestive or lightly descriptive**, not a riddle and not a stack of near-identical descriptives.

If several candidates are the same pattern with swapped nouns (place + audience, job + city), pick one and drop the rest. Similar descriptives confuse recall.

Stop after **two short slates**. A third only if the founder asks and none of the first set felt like them. Then stop: working title or unnamed until issue 1.

## How to talk about it

- Say “working title,” “we’ll use this for now,” or “this can change after the first issue.”
- Do not say “provisional default.”
- Do not present trademark, domain, or handle clearance you have not done. A noisy search is an editorial screen, not legal advice.
- Do not justify a title with rhyme studies or vowel-sound symbolism.
- Do not write a persona into the name from one thin voice signal.

## What the research supports

Use this only if the founder asks why, or to choose between a clear title and a clever one. Do not recite it.

- Meaningful names (descriptive and suggestive) are liked more and recalled more easily than arbitrary or made-up names on first exposure. About one in five names was recalled the next day in the main study (Kohli & Suri, 2000).
- Pure descriptive names confuse each other when several look-alikes sit in the same category. Suggestive names are cleaner when recalled (Kohli & Suri, 2000).
- Small-budget products should start descriptive or suggestive. Clever unrelated names need spend, and they do not automatically catch up without that exposure (Kohli & Suri, 2000; Kohli & Harich, 2005).
- Trademark law is the same ladder: descriptive is easy to explain and hard to own; coined is easy to own and expensive to explain; suggestive is the usual middle (USPTO spectrum).
- Concrete, imageable names help memory, including when they stick to the wrong category (de Lencastre, Machado & Costa, 2023).
- Rhyme research is about slogans, not publication titles (Filkuková & Klempe, 2013). Do not use it to pick a name.
- Newsletter-platform advice is weaker: keep it short and clear; put the place in the title if the publication is local; use the founder’s name only if they *are* the product (beehiiv). Discovery favors a boring descriptive name over a metaphor (operator reports, not trials).

There is no study that randomly assigns newsletter titles and measures subscribers, opens, or forwards. Do not claim a name will grow the list.

## Output shape

When naming is the job:

1. State the relationship branch (business / no business).
2. Recommend one working title and why, in one or two sentences.
3. Offer at most two alternatives plus Other.
4. Give each a one-line risk, not a lockup essay.
5. Ask which to use for now, and continue to the next launch gate.

If they dislike the set, offer one more slate or defer. Do not keep inventing worlds.
