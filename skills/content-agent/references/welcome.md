# Welcome

Load this when first-issue work starts, or when the welcome/capture gate is current. Do not open a welcome workshop during kickoff.

New subscribers do **not** automatically receive the latest issue. Unless the founder intentionally creates that experience, the welcome is what they get until the next send. Raise that fact as soon as the first issue is in motion. It may be critical; it may not. The founder decides. Do not wait until after the draft to mention it.

Recommend one easy path. Let them redirect.

## Lean policy

**Lean default: one welcome email.** Use the built-in welcome email on every plan unless a short automation has a distinct reader benefit and material that already exists.

Two facts still matter:

1. **Can they use automations?** The built-in welcome email exists on every beehiiv plan, including free Launch. Automations are on paid plans (Scale and above). If the plan is unknown, ask once. Do not recommend an automation a free account cannot run.
2. **Would a sequence materially help this reader?** Cadence alone is not enough. A short automation is optional only when the founder has paid access, **existing source material**, and a **distinct reader benefit** that one welcome email cannot provide. Do not invent future issues or build a funnel to fill silence.

## Easy defaults

- **Free / Launch:** one built-in welcome email. Do not pitch a series they cannot send.
- **Paid, and weekly or more:** one welcome. Stop there unless a separate follow-up has an identified reader job and existing material.
- **Paid, and not sending regularly yet:** still start with one welcome. A short automation of complete useful units is optional only when the material already exists and the benefit is explicit. Irregular cadence by itself does not justify more infrastructure.

If they want something else, take the direction. Offer two alternatives plus Other only when the choice is still open.

If a Signed Up automation is on, turn the preset welcome email **off**. Both at once sends two welcomes.

## What the first email does

The first welcome has one job: they are glad they signed up and sure what this is.

- who it is for;
- what it is;
- cadence, honestly;
- one promise;
- one useful thing they can use now;
- a quiet disclosure if a business is attached;
- one small reply, not a quiz.

It should not sell, invent a community, preview unreleased product, or collect a pile of onboarding data.

Later emails in an approved short sequence are complete useful units from material that already exists. They are not fake future issues.

## How to talk about it

Bring it up when first-issue work starts:

> New subscribers will not get this issue automatically. We should decide the welcome now — it might be the only thing they receive until you send.

Then give the lean recommendation for their plan and actual material, and continue. Draft the welcome in chat when they want it. Loading it into beehiiv waits on their account.
