# Welcome

Load this when first-issue work starts, or when the welcome/capture gate is current. Do not open a welcome workshop during kickoff.

New subscribers do **not** automatically receive the latest issue. Unless the founder builds that into an automation each time, the welcome is what they get until the next send. Raise that fact as soon as the first issue is in motion. It may be critical; it may not. The founder decides. Do not wait until after the draft to mention it.

Recommend one easy path. Let them redirect.

## What decides the rec

Two facts, in this order:

1. **Can they use automations?** The built-in welcome email exists on every beehiiv plan, including free Launch. Automations are on paid plans (Scale and above). If the plan is unknown, ask once. Do not recommend an automation a free account cannot run.
2. **Will readers get regular issues soon?** If they are collecting signups before a weekly-or-faster cadence, the welcome series *is* the publication for a while. If they will send weekly or more, keep the welcome short so it does not compete with the issue.

## Easy defaults

- **Free / Launch:** one built-in welcome email. That is the only option. Do not pitch a series they cannot send.
- **Paid, and weekly or more:** one welcome. Optional: one follow-up about two weeks later. Stop there.
- **Paid, and not sending regularly yet:** recommend a longer series as a **low-risk way to start** — introduce the writer and the newsletter while the list is quiet. It is a recommendation, not a requirement. They may feel self-conscious about automation; do not treat it as infrastructure they must build. One welcome email is a fine alternative. Still bounded. Not a funnel, not invented future issues.

If they want something else, take the direction. Offer two alternatives plus Other only when the choice is still open.

If a Signed Up automation is on, turn the preset welcome email **off**. Both at once sends two welcomes.

## What the emails do

The first welcome has one job: they are glad they signed up and sure what this is.

- who it is for
- what it is
- cadence, honestly
- one promise
- one useful thing they can use now
- a quiet disclosure if a business is attached
- one small reply, not a quiz

It should not sell, invent a community, preview unreleased product, or collect a pile of onboarding data.

Later emails in a series are complete useful units from material that already exists. They are not fake future issues.

## How to talk about it

Bring it up when first-issue work starts:

> New subscribers will not get this issue automatically. We should decide the welcome now — it might be the only thing they receive until you send.

Then give the easy rec for their plan and cadence, and continue. If they are not sending yet, say the series is optional — a simple way to introduce the newsletter until issues start — not something they have to set up to be legitimate. Draft the welcome in chat when they want it. Loading it into beehiiv waits on their account.
