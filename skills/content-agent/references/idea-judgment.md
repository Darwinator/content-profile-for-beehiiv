# Idea Judgment

The agent's first editorial responsibility is selection, including restraint. Recency, business importance, and ease of drafting do not by themselves make an idea worth publishing.

## Publishability gates

An opportunity should normally pass all five gates before becoming a full issue.

1. **Reader relevance:** A defined reader has a real question, decision, risk, or opportunity.
2. **Useful payoff:** The issue can create an independent benefit, not merely announce something.
3. **Founder proximity:** The founder has credible experience, evidence, access, or a clearly labeled point of view.
4. **Sufficient substance:** There is enough specificity, tension, demonstration, or evidence to earn attention.
5. **Distinct timing:** It is not an unnecessary repeat, and there is a reason to develop it now.

A strong score on one gate does not compensate for a fatal failure on another.

## Internal candidate review

Before returning recommendations, test each candidate against:

- **Reader sentence:** “This helps [reader] who is trying to [job] by showing [payoff].”
- **Evidence sentence:** “We can responsibly support it with [source IDs], while [gap] remains.”
- **Difference sentence:** “Unlike prior issue [record], this focuses on [new contribution].”
- **Commercial sentence:** “It remains useful without buying, while the business connection is [transparent relationship].”
- **Restraint sentence:** “The strongest reason not to publish this now is [reason].”

If these cannot be stated plainly, develop or reject the candidate before proposing it.

## Possible treatments

Not every useful item needs a full issue.

- **Full issue:** enough reader payoff and evidence to sustain a complete argument or story.
- **Section:** useful supporting material inside a broader issue.
- **Short note:** one bounded observation with honest scope.
- **Question to investigate:** promising but missing evidence or clarity.
- **Wait:** timing or source material is incomplete.
- **Reject:** self-serving, repetitive, unsupported, confidential, or not useful enough.

## The Next Three output

Return no more than three opportunities and fewer when appropriate. For each include:

1. working angle, not just topic;
2. reader and situation;
3. promised payoff;
4. founder proximity;
5. evidence available and missing;
6. overlap check;
7. recommended treatment;
8. main editorial risk.

Then include **Not now** with the most tempting rejected material and the reason. This makes restraint inspectable and helps the founder improve future inputs.

## Common false positives

- A launch, feature, hire, milestone, or completed task with no independent reader lesson.
- A customer story whose permission or representative value is unclear.
- A broad trend the founder can only summarize generically.
- A contrarian claim selected for novelty without evidence.
- A repeat of a successful issue with no new contribution.
- Advice that primarily creates demand for the founder's offer.
- An idea that could be written immediately but would not be missed if absent.

## Selection decision

Ask the founder to select, reject, combine, or redirect. Record both the choice and concise reason. Rejection is durable context, not a failure to hide.
